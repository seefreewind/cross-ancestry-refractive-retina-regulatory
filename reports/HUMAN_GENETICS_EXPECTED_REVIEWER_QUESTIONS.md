# Expected reviewer questions and prepared answers

## 1. Why not perform experimental validation?

The study tests a statistical genetic-architecture question: whether ancestry-associated retinal DARs concentrate EUR-EAS GWAS effect heterogeneity for refractive error. Experimental validation would be appropriate for locus-specific mechanisms, but the frozen result does not identify a large global DAR enrichment or prioritize a specific causal locus.

## 2. Why only EUR and EAS?

EUR-EAS was the frozen inference unit for the harmonized GWAS, ancestry-matched LDSC and S-LDXR framework. Extending to additional ancestries would require a new prespecified analysis rather than a post-primary expansion.

## 3. Why no AFR analysis?

AFR resources were catalogued during setup, but the manuscript-stage formal endpoint was frozen for EUR-EAS. The paper should not generalize its conclusion to AFR or admixed populations.

## 4. How should GCORSQ > 1 be interpreted?

The genome-wide GCORSQ estimate was 1.009945 ± 0.108276. This is boundary-adjacent behavior of an unbounded estimator near high sharing, not evidence that the underlying biological parameter exceeds its natural boundary.

## 5. Why use DARs?

Ancestry-associated retinal DARs provide a direct annotation for testing whether ancestry-associated retinal regulatory variation corresponds to ancestry-divergent refractive-error genetic effects.

## 6. Was the DAR analysis underpowered?

It was powered to detect large enrichment but not modest enrichment. The primary DAR set contained 1,232 SNPs; the 80% and 90% minimum detectable ORs were 1.397 and 1.467.

## 7. Why choose the top 5% endpoint?

The top 5% chi2_het endpoint was the frozen primary binary endpoint. Top 1% and top 10% thresholds were reported only as sensitivity endpoints.

## 8. Is the matched non-DAR comparator truly ancestry stable?

No. It is an operational comparator made from matched non-DAR retinal OCR SNPs. The manuscript should not imply that this comparator is invariant across ancestries.

## 9. How was GWAS genome-build uncertainty handled?

The released GWAS files did not explicitly report assembly. Coordinates were independently adjudicated against dbSNP Build 151 and anchored to a frozen GRCh37.p13 framework.

## 10. Could sample overlap affect heterogeneity statistics?

The heterogeneity statistic treats EUR and EAS GWAS as independent. Any residual cross-ancestry overlap is a limitation and could affect variance estimates, but it does not create evidence for DAR enrichment in the frozen analysis.

## 11. Could LD drive the result?

Matched permutation accounted for LD-score strata, and block-level robustness adjusted for block SNP count, mean MAF, mean baseline LD score and retinal OCR density. LD-pruned analyses were too underpowered for directional interpretation because r2 < 0.1 retained only 9 DAR SNPs.

## 12. Why not provide locus-level mechanisms?

The primary result did not support a large global DAR enrichment. Adding locus, CRE-to-gene, motif or pathway narratives after that result would risk post hoc discovery.

## 13. How does this differ from Cheng 2026?

Cheng 2026 expanded multi-ancestry refractive-error association discovery and prediction. This manuscript tests whether retinal ancestry-associated regulatory variation concentrates cross-ancestry effect heterogeneity.

## 14. How does this differ from the Human Retina Cell Atlas?

HRCA mapped retinal cell and chromatin landscapes and reported ancestry-associated accessibility variation. This manuscript tests the relationship between those annotations and cross-ancestry refractive-error GWAS effect divergence.

## 15. Is P > 0.05 evidence for sharing?

No. The manuscript does not use nonsignificance alone as proof of equivalence. It combines genome-wide and retinal OCR S-LDXR, primary effect estimate, confidence interval, matched permutation, block-level robustness and power bounds.

## 16. Does the study exclude all ancestry-related regulatory effects?

No. It excludes a large systematic DAR enrichment under the frozen top 5% endpoint. Modest, locus-specific, rare-variant, developmental-stage-specific and gene-environment effects remain possible.

## 17. Why is Human Genetics the right venue?

The manuscript asks a cross-ancestry human genetics question using GWAS, LD-score methods, ancestry terminology and genetic-architecture interpretation. Retinal annotations are used to test a genetic hypothesis rather than to claim a retinal mechanism.
