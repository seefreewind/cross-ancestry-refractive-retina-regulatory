# Human Genetics editor risk audit

Status: NO NEW EXPERIMENTS REQUIRED BEFORE FIRST SUBMISSION

## Major editorial risks and mitigations

1. Risk: The study may be viewed as a negative-result paper.
   Mitigation: Frame the manuscript around a precise human genetics question and emphasize the bounded inference: strong shared genome-wide architecture plus no evidence for large DAR enrichment.

2. Risk: Editors may see the work as secondary data integration.
   Mitigation: Highlight reference anchoring, S-LDXR, matched heterogeneity testing, permutation, block robustness and power bounds as the core methodological contribution.

3. Risk: Human Genetics may not want a retinal mechanism paper.
   Mitigation: Keep the paper positioned as cross-ancestry genetic architecture and statistical genetics. Do not overbuild retinal mechanism claims.

4. Risk: DAR sample size is small.
   Mitigation: State underpowering clearly, avoid trend language from LD-pruned results, and use effect-size bounds to explain what is and is not excluded.

5. Risk: GCORSQ > 1 may be misunderstood.
   Mitigation: Explain that S-LDXR GCORSQ is an unbounded estimator near the boundary and should not be interpreted as exceeding the biological parameter boundary.

6. Risk: Genome-build uncertainty could concern reviewers.
   Mitigation: Describe the released-file limitation transparently and present dbSNP Build 151 adjudication plus frozen GRCh37.p13 anchoring as a reproducibility safeguard.

7. Risk: Matched non-DAR retinal OCRs could be read as truly ancestry-stable.
   Mitigation: Call them an operational comparator only; avoid implying that the comparator is invariant across ancestries.

8. Risk: Lack of AFR or other ancestries may limit scope.
   Mitigation: State that EUR-EAS was the frozen inference unit and do not generalize to other populations.

9. Risk: Lack of experimental validation may be criticized.
   Mitigation: Clarify that the endpoint is statistical enrichment of cross-ancestry effect heterogeneity, not causal regulatory mechanism.

10. Risk: The manuscript may seem less novel than Cheng 2026 or HRCA.
    Mitigation: Use the novelty sentence from the novelty audit and keep “beyond prior work” focused on the cross-layer test.

## Current recommendation

Proceed with manuscript refinement, figure/table assembly and reference verification. No new discovery analysis, locus fishing, motif analysis, TWAS, SMR, MR, PRS or experimental validation is required before first submission.
