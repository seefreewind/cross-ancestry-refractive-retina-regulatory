# Discussion v1.2 to v1.3 changelog

## Conceptual restructuring

- Rebuilt the Discussion around the central question: why ancestry-associated retinal molecular regulatory variation can coexist with predominantly shared cross-ancestry genetic architecture for refractive error.
- Changed the opening from a result-by-result recap to a direct conceptual answer.
- Reframed the main interpretation as separation between molecular regulatory variation and association-effect heterogeneity.

## Literature added

- Added Wang et al. 2024 as the key contrasting AJHG study.
- Reused Hu et al. 2025 and Lu et al. 2025, already added in manuscript v1.3, to support broad sharing plus local heterogeneity across complex-trait and molecular architectures.
- Cheng et al. 2026 and Li et al. 2026 remain the two project-defining sources.

## Literature removed

- No core reference was removed from the manuscript.
- Wingo et al. 2025 was verified but not added to the Discussion because the brain pQTL setting is less directly tied to retinal chromatin accessibility.

## Mechanisms added

- Added a cautious explanatory framework distinguishing chromatin accessibility as a molecular phenotype from marginal GWAS association effects.
- Added restrained discussion of regulatory redundancy, distributed polygenicity, developmental compensation and context dependence as possible explanations.
- Added explicit statement that global sharing and local ancestry specificity are not competing models.

## Strengths compressed

- Compressed prior audit-style strengths into a short methodological-advance paragraph focused on the tissue-matched comparator and nested design.
- Removed extended discussion of reference anchoring, row-level QC and MAF-threshold audit from the Discussion emphasis; these remain in Methods/Supplement/report files.

## Limitations regrouped

- Reorganized limitations into three impact-oriented themes: annotation power/misclassification, effect-scale/study-design constraints and scope/generalizability.
- Explained how each limitation could affect inference rather than listing weaknesses.

## Clinical/scientific implications revised

- Removed clinical-practice and precision-medicine implications.
- Added the scientific implication that ancestry-associated molecular annotations should be treated as hypotheses about trait heterogeneity rather than evidence of heterogeneity by themselves.
- Added a bounded statement about PRS portability: reduced portability should not automatically be attributed to ancestry-associated retinal chromatin differences.

## Claims softened/strengthened

- Strengthened the conceptual claim that molecular ancestry variation and ancestry-divergent complex-trait effects are distinct layers.
- Softened mechanistic explanations to “may,” “could” and “is consistent with.”
- Preserved the statistical boundary: large OR = 1.5 enrichment is unlikely under the tested endpoint, while modest or local effects remain possible.
