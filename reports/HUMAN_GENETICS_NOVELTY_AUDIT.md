# Human Genetics novelty audit

## Q1. What is new beyond the 2026 multi-ancestry refractive-error GWAS?

The 2026 multi-ancestry refractive-error GWAS expanded association discovery and polygenic prediction. This manuscript asks a different question: whether ancestry-associated retinal regulatory regions preferentially harbor SNPs with ancestry-divergent EUR-EAS genetic effects on refractive error. The novelty is the matched regulatory-heterogeneity estimand, not a larger GWAS locus catalog.

## Q2. What is new beyond the Human Retina Cell Atlas?

The Human Retina Cell Atlas documented transcriptomic and chromatin-accessibility landscapes, including ancestry-associated retinal chromatin variation. This manuscript tests whether those ancestry-associated retinal regulatory annotations translate into enriched cross-ancestry GWAS effect heterogeneity for refractive error.

## Q3. Why is this not merely data integration?

The analysis uses a prespecified statistical question, reference anchoring, ancestry-specific LDSC, cross-population S-LDXR, a frozen DAR-vs-matched-non-DAR retinal OCR comparator, matched permutation, block-level robustness and effect-size bounds. These components convert public resources into a reproducible genetic-architecture test.

## Q4. Why is a negative DAR result informative?

The result is bounded. The primary estimate was close to one, the matched permutation was unsupported, block-level adjustment did not change interpretation, and the confidence interval was inconsistent with OR ≥ 1.5. The manuscript also states that modest enrichment remains possible, preserving the correct uncertainty.

## Novelty sentence for manuscript or cover letter

“Rather than expanding the refractive-error locus catalog, this study tests whether ancestry-associated retinal regulatory variation marks regions of ancestry-divergent genetic effects, and finds a bounded negative result within a strongly shared EUR-EAS architecture.”
