# Cover letter: Human Genetics

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, “Shared cross-ancestry genetic architecture of refractive error across retinal regulatory regions,” for consideration as a Research Article in *Human Genetics*.

This study addresses a targeted question in human and statistical genetics: does ancestry-associated molecular regulatory variation necessarily translate into ancestry-divergent genetic effects on a complex trait? We used refractive error as a model ocular trait and integrated EUR and EAS refractive-error GWAS summary statistics with human retinal open chromatin annotations and ancestry-associated differentially accessible retinal regions.

The manuscript reports three main findings. First, ancestry-matched LDSC and genome-wide S-LDXR supported predominantly shared EUR-EAS common-variant architecture for refractive error, with genome-wide GCORSQ = 1.010 ± 0.108 across 3,112,573 formal SNPs. Second, broad retinal open chromatin regions showed a compatible shared-architecture pattern, with all-retinal-OCR GCORSQ = 0.935 ± 0.101. Third, ancestry-associated retinal DARs did not show detectable excess EUR-EAS effect heterogeneity compared with matched non-DAR retinal OCRs. In the frozen primary top 5% heterogeneity endpoint, the DAR enrichment estimate was OR = 1.119 (95% CI 0.867–1.423, P = 0.367), with matched permutation empirical P = 0.334. The confidence interval was inconsistent with a large enrichment of OR ≥ 1.5, while modest regulatory heterogeneity remains possible.

We believe the manuscript fits *Human Genetics* because it is framed as a cross-ancestry genetic-architecture study rather than a retinal mechanism or cell-type discovery paper. Its novelty lies beyond recent multi-ancestry refractive-error GWAS and the Human Retina Cell Atlas: those studies characterized association architecture and retinal regulatory variation, whereas the present study directly tests whether ancestry-associated retinal regulatory variation concentrates ancestry-divergent genetic effects. The result is a rigorously bounded negative regulatory-enrichment result within a strongly shared cross-ancestry architecture.

All analyses use public GWAS, retinal annotation and reference resources, with frozen configuration files, reproducible harmonization, reference anchoring and figure-ready tables prepared for repository release before submission.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors will approve the submitted version.
